Anatomy of a language model

It doesn't plan a sentence. It picks one next word, from odds it works out fresh each time, and then does it again.

The parts

Point at a part to find it in the figure; click it to step the machine to it.

  1. 01

    Context

    Everything written so far, cut into tokens: often whole words, sometimes pieces of words. Each token is turned into a long list of numbers (its embedding), and that list drops into its own column.

  2. 02

    Layers

    A stack of identical layers. Every column passes through every layer, and each layer updates what the column holds, so a word's numbers come to mean that word in this sentence. Real models stack dozens of layers, and each column carries thousands of numbers.

  3. 03

    Attention

    Inside each layer, a column takes in information from the columns before it, more from some than others. These threads show how much the last word takes from each earlier one: one layer leans on 'on', another on the subject or the verb. A word can only look back, which is why a model writes left to right. Real layers run many of these patterns side by side (heads); the weights here are illustrative.

  4. 04

    Scores

    Only the last column is used to predict what comes next. The scoring head compares its numbers with every token in the vocabulary, tens of thousands of them, and gives each a score (a logit). Six are shown. A score is not a probability yet: it can be any number, even negative.

  5. 05

    Temperature

    Softmax turns scores into odds that add up to 100%. Before that, every score is divided by the temperature. Below 1 the odds sharpen towards the top word; above 1 they flatten towards an even spread.

  6. 06

    Sampler

    The odds are laid end to end along a tape, so each word gets a length equal to its chance. A random number picks a point, and the word under it is written next. Then it joins the context, and the whole machine runs again for the word after.
