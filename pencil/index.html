Anatomy of a pencil

A wooden pencil looks like one thing. It's five, each solving a problem the others can't.

The parts

  1. Core

    Not lead. It's graphite mixed with clay and fired. More clay makes a harder, lighter line (the H grades), and more graphite a softer, darker one (the B grades). HB sits in the middle.

  2. Casing

    Two slats of cedar, picked because it sharpens cleanly without splitting. Each slat has a groove, the core is glued between them, and the sandwich is cut into pencils.

  3. Finish

    Several coats of lacquer seal the wood against moisture and give the grip its feel. The hexagon keeps the pencil from rolling off the desk.

  4. Ferrule

    A crimped aluminium band that joins two materials that won't glue well: wood and rubber. The wood is turned down to a shoulder so the band sits flush.

  5. Eraser

    A plug of rubber or vinyl. It works by being slightly stickier than paper, so graphite particles cling to it instead.
