Anatomy of TITLE

A wooden pencil looks like one thing. It's actually five materials, each solving a different problem.

The parts

  1. Core

    Not lead. It's graphite mixed with clay and fired. More clay makes a harder, lighter line (the H grades). More graphite makes a softer, darker one (the B grades).

  2. Casing

    Usually cedar, picked because it sharpens cleanly without splintering. The core is glued between two grooved slats, then the sandwich is cut into individual pencils.

  3. Finish

    Several coats of lacquer seal the wood against moisture and give the grip its feel. The hexagonal shape stops the pencil rolling off the desk.

  4. Ferrule

    A crimped metal band, usually aluminum, that joins two materials that won't glue well: wood and rubber.

  5. Eraser

    A soft rubber or vinyl plug. It works by being slightly stickier than paper, so graphite particles cling to it instead.

The design idea: every part exists because one material couldn't do two jobs. Graphite can't be held, wood can't mark, and rubber can't be glued to wood.
